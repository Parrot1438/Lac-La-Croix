  <section id="spirit-horses" class="container">
    <span class="section-eyebrow">Visual Anchor &amp; Cultural Survival</span>
    <h2 class="section-title">The 1977 Ojibwe Spirit Horse Rescue</h2>
    <div class="spotlight-card">
      <div class="spotlight-art">
        <div class="horse-icon">🐎</div>
        <h3>Lac La Croix Indigenous Pony</h3>
        <p style="color:var(--accent-sage); font-size:0.85rem; margin-top:0.5rem;">Only 4 surviving mares remained in 1977</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <p>The Lac La Croix Indigenous Pony is the only native Canadian forest horse breed, uniquely adapted to the boreal climate with specialized nasal flaps that pre-warm arctic air and dense hooves built for navigating granite rock.</p>
        <p style="margin-top:1rem;">In 1977, facing government eradication orders, courageous community members smuggled the last four surviving mares across the frozen ice of Lac La Croix under the cover of night into Minnesota. Today, the herd has revitalized and returned to Lac La Croix, where they are central to youth mental health and equine-assisted cultural healing.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>


  <section id="inquiry" class="container">
    <span class="section-eyebrow">Academic Research</span>
    <h2 class="section-title">Core Inquiry Questions</h2>
    <div class="inquiry-grid">
      <div class="inquiry-card">
        <span class="inquiry-num">Inquiry 01</span>
        <h3>Relationship with the Land</h3>
        <p class="inquiry-synthesis">For the Gakijiwanong Anishinaabe Nation, land (Aki) and water (Nibi) are living relatives, not property to be bought or sold. In their creation story, humans were created last—making humanity the youngest, most dependent sibling in creation. Traditional law (Inakonigewin) focuses on respect, reciprocity, and maintaining ecological balance in the Canadian Shield rather than resource exhaustion.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="inquiry-card">
        <span class="inquiry-num">Inquiry 02</span>
        <h3>Traditional Practices &amp; Stewardship</h3>
        <p class="inquiry-synthesis">Connection to the land is demonstrated through daily protocols like offering asemaa (sacred tobacco) before taking resources. The wild rice (manoomin) harvest uses push-poles and cedar knockers that gather grain while deliberately returning seeds to the muddy waters, simultaneously replanting next year's crop and feeding migratory birds. Red ochre rock paintings on granite cliffs mark sacred offerings to water spirits before lake crossings.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="inquiry-card">
        <span class="inquiry-num">Inquiry 03</span>
        <h3>Culture, Clans &amp; Identity</h3>
        <p class="inquiry-synthesis">The Canadian Shield environment forms the bedrock of community identity. The clan system (Doodemag) assigns leadership and community responsibilities based on local wildlife (e.g., Bear clan for defense and medicine). The seasonal calendar names months after ecological transitions, such as the Freezing Over Moon. The Ojibwe Spirit Horse stands as a living cultural symbol of endurance and youth wellness.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="inquiry-card">
        <span class="inquiry-num">Inquiry 04</span>
        <h3>Treaties, Injustice &amp; Restitution</h3>
        <p class="inquiry-synthesis">After signing Treaty 3 in 1873/1874, the community's Sturgeon Lake Reserve was unilaterally dissolved in 1915 by the Ontario government to create Quetico Provincial Park without consent. Families were evicted and criminalized for hunting. Decades of legal resistance led to a 1994 Co-Existence Agreement with a provincial apology, and a historic $131.3 million land claim settlement in May 2023. Ongoing threats include cross-border copper mining in Minnesota.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="inquiry-card">
        <span class="inquiry-num">Inquiry 05</span>
        <h3>Contemporary Cultural Revitalization</h3>
        <p class="inquiry-synthesis">Lac La Croix keeps its culture vibrant through immersive seasonal bush camps, where youth learn directly from Elders how to smoke fish, tan hides, and speak Anishinaabemowin. Band members manage the Quetico Park entry station, safeguarding ancestral portages and pictographs. Community youth also express pride through modern music, such as the track and video "Firemakers."</p>
      </div>
      <div class="inquiry-card">
        <span class="inquiry-num">Inquiry 06</span>
        <h3>Paradigms for Canada &amp; Two-Eyed Seeing</h3>
        <p class="inquiry-synthesis">Broader society can learn three vital lessons: the Seven Generations principle (ensuring decisions protect descendants seven generations ahead), dismantling the myth of "untouched wilderness" by recognizing Indigenous land management, and embracing Two-Eyed Seeing—uniting traditional Indigenous ecological knowledge with modern scientific testing to protect watersheds.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

        <div class="timeline-card">
          <h4>Treaty #3 Signed</h4>
          <p>Treaty 3 signed with the Crown, guaranteeing reserve lands at Sturgeon Lake and perpetual hunting and fishing rights.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="timeline-node">
        <div class="timeline-date">1909 – 1915</div>
        <div class="timeline-card">
          <h4>Quetico Park Land Grab</h4>
          <p>Ontario unilaterally passes legislation erasing the Sturgeon Lake Reserve to create Quetico Provincial Park; hunting is banned and members are evicted.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="timeline-node">
        <div class="timeline-date">1977</div>
        <div class="timeline-card">
          <h4>The Spirit Horse Rescue</h4>
          <p>The last four surviving Lac La Croix Indigenous Ponies are smuggled across a frozen lake into Minnesota to save them from government cull orders.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="timeline-node">
        <div class="timeline-date">1994</div>
        <div class="timeline-card">
          <h4>Quetico Co-Existence Agreement</h4>
          <p>Ontario formally apologizes for the 1915 expropriation and signs a co-existence agreement permitting traditional motorboat guiding businesses.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="timeline-node">
        <div class="timeline-date">MAY 2023</div>
        <div class="timeline-card">
          <h4>$131.3 Million Settlement</h4>
          <p>A landmark tripartite settlement with Canada and Ontario resolves the historic Sturgeon Lake reserve claim, funding land re-acquisition.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
